Math & Theory

Calculated using Onderdonk's Equation, which models the adiabatic heating of copper until it reaches its melting point (1083 °C).

$I = A \cdot \sqrt{ \frac{\log_{10}\left(\frac{\Delta T}{274 + T_{amb}} + 1\right)}{33 \cdot t} }$

Where:

  • $I$ = Fusing current (Amps)
  • $A$ = Trace cross-sectional area in circular mils (1 sq mil = 1.273 circular mils)
  • $\Delta T = 1083 - T_{amb}$ (Temperature rise to melt copper)
  • $t$ = Time duration of the current pulse (seconds)
