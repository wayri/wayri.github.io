Math & Theory

Type II Compensator: Provides a single zero and two poles (one at origin). Used for current-mode control.

\( H(s) = \frac{1 + s R_2 C_2}{s R_1 (C_1 + C_2) (1 + s R_2 \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2})} \)

\( F_{z1} = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot R_2 \cdot C_2} \)

\( F_{p1} = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot R_2 \cdot \left(\frac{C_1 \cdot C_2}{C_1 + C_2}\right)} \)

Design Logic: Place \(F_{z1}\) slightly below the LC resonance. Place \(F_{p1}\) at the ESR zero of the output capacitor to cancel it.

Type III Compensator: Provides two zeros and three poles. Used for voltage-mode control.

\( H(s) = \frac{(1 + s R_2 C_2) (1 + s (R_1 + R_3) C_3)}{s R_1 (C_1 + C_2) (1 + s R_2 \frac{C_1 C_2}{C_1 + C_2}) (1 + s R_3 C_3)} \)

\( F_{z1} = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot R_2 \cdot C_2} \)

\( F_{z2} = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot (R_1 + R_3) \cdot C_3} \)

\( F_{p1} = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot R_2 \cdot \left(\frac{C_1 \cdot C_2}{C_1 + C_2}\right)} \)

\( F_{p2} = \frac{1}{2\pi \cdot R_3 \cdot C_3} \)

Design Logic: Place both zeros (\(F_{z1}\), \(F_{z2}\)) near the double LC resonance to offset the -40dB/dec drop and -180° phase shift. Place one pole (\(F_{p1}\)) at the ESR zero and the other (\(F_{p2}\)) at half the switching frequency to attenuate high-frequency switching noise.
