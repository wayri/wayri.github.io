8P8C contacts: preserve the actual twisted pairs

The contact pairs are 1–2, 3–6, 4–5, 7–8. Pins 3 and 6 belong together even though they are not adjacent. For conventional 10/100 MDI, 1–2 is TX and 3–6 is RX; MDI-X reverses those roles. Gigabit BASE-T uses all four pairs. Auto-MDI-X support does not excuse a split pair.

Confirm connector contact numbers from its drawing; solder-side and mating-side views mirror each other. T568A and T568B are cable color assignments, not alternative PHY pinouts. Use the PHY and magnetics drawings to map PCB nets to cable contacts.

Layout & bring-up checklist

  • Use the PHY’s MDI differential-impedance target and length/skew limits; 100 Ω is common for copper MDI.
  • Check whether termination is internal before adding resistors. Use the specified magnetics, center-tap wiring and common-mode network.
  • Keep pairs balanced with a continuous reference on the PHY side. Apply the manufacturer’s copper keep-outs under the isolation barrier and magnetics.
  • Place the PHY clock, bias resistor and decoupling close to their pins; separate noisy switching nodes.
  • Review ESD paths, shield/chassis connection and isolation spacing as a system. PoE requires its own compliant power interface.
  • For a dead link: verify rails, reset, clock, straps, MDIO identity, negotiation state and cable pairs before changing RGMII delay.
